# Test of 8 September 2026 — what it showed

Buoy 2, KIM1 `276f235` (CLS deviceRef 217459), rooftop at Vilanova
41.224 N 1.736 E, good antenna, deployed 06:40 UTC. First field run of the pass
**planner** (whole-day pass table, merged sessions, yield filter).

Files here: `LogFile.txt` (151 logged transmissions), `tx.csv` (the `TX;` lines
extracted), `cls_receptions.json` (CLS, deduplicated by `deviceMsgUid`),
`expected_plan.txt` (written the night before, so the comparison is a diff and
not an interpretation).

Reproduce with `tools/tx_log.ps1`, `tools/cls.sh` and `tools/join_cls.ps1`.

---

## The planner works

**14 of 14 sessions exactly as pre-registered**, including both stacked ones.
Zero SPP errors, zero `MSG_ERR`, zero overlaps, zero LOWPWR drops, zero short
light sleeps. Two GPS fixes failed, both in the first minutes after deployment —
a cold start.

**26 of 26 receptions fell inside a predicted window with the predicted
satellite.** The one apparent exception, 10:27:08, is 11 s past a session's
predicted end: that is the deliberate round-up in `SendGPSMessage`, and it
arrived — the decision is vindicated.

**The GPS message lands at 98 % of the pass's peak elevation**, 13 times out of
13, between 92 % and 99 %. The centring works as designed.

**The buoy starts transmitting when the pass opens.** The GPS message goes out
after exactly `ceil(fileSendingTime/30)` data lines, so its position reconstructs
when the burst began: **+9 s, −19 s and +4 s** across three sessions.
`TIME_LESS_BEFORE_AWAKENING = 30` was correctly sized for that purpose.

**Stacking pays, observed directly.** In the merged O3(78°)+3A(45°) session the
transmission at 12:20:50 was received by *both satellites at once* — the union
probability the bench scoring was corrected to model on 3 Sep.

## Reception is a third of the published benchmark

**25 of 151 transmitted = 16.6 %.** de la Vega et al. report ~50 % per message
sent in the current constellation and ~64 % in the early tests. Their 62.8 %
"unique reception" is a per-line figure *without* repetition; the ~90-98 % is
image reconstruction *with* it. So we are low by a factor of three, and that is
the open question.

Ruled out with our own data:

- **Message length.** GPS messages are 184 bits and data lines 248; both received
  at 16-17 %.
- **The 13 s cadence bug.** Message 3 of every session carries a normal 30 s gap
  and was lost just as reliably as message 2.
- **Elevation, within 30-89°.** For messages 4 onward: 21 % / 19 % / 21 % across
  30-49, 50-69 and 70-89 degrees. Flat.
- **Software drift since `main`.** `maskGPS` is byte-identical (the CRC branch was
  already compiled out), the only `KIM.cpp` change is naming the UART pins, which
  is a no-op on core 2.0.14, and the payload reaches the module untouched at the
  same lengths.

## The first three messages of every session are never received

**0 of 45.** And the losses are not symmetric across a geometrically symmetric
pass:

```
entry half   68 sent    3 heard     4 %
exit half    83 sent   22 heard    27 %
```

The test that separates the candidates: **time from first transmission to first
reception is 184 s, and does not move with geometry** — 183 s for passes peaking
at 25-49°, 186 s for 50-89°. Doppler would depend on the pass. A transmitter
settling after its rail comes back does not, and `goToSleep()` cuts `GPS_KIM`
between DM wakes, so the module starts cold in every one of the day's sessions.
Consistent with August looking better: FRM leaves the module powered throughout.

Excluding that dead opening, reception is **25 of 106 = 23.6 %**.

Not settled — ten sessions with receptions, four against six in the geometry
split. The low-elevation campaign is the test.

## Kinéis against the legacy satellites: not conclusive

August says 1.16×, September 1.8×, and the ratio is small next to the variance
between individual satellites — 2D gave 55 % and 2E gave 0 % the same day at
almost the same peak elevation. The one clean paired observation, O3 and 3A
overhead together receiving the *same* 13 transmissions, went **2 against 4 in
the Kinéis satellite's favour** despite 33° less elevation. Signal levels are
identical between fleets (−130.3 vs −130.7 dBm), so when a Kinéis satellite hears
us it hears us just as well.

MetOp-B is consistently the worst — 0 receptions across three days of continuous
August transmission, 1 of 11 in September at the weakest level recorded — but the
numbers do not justify dropping a satellite from the AOP.

## What changed in the firmware because of this

| commit | change |
|---|---|
| `9ef2d34` | Cadence clock armed at the first transmission. It was measuring from `millis() = 0`, which included the boot and GPS fix, so the second message of every session went out ~13 s after the first instead of 30 — under the minimum spacing CLS accepts. Verified on hardware: consecutive messages now 30.0 s apart. |
| `470b9b2` | `TIME_LESS_BEFORE_AWAKENING` 30 → 120 s, so the transmitter has longer powered before it has to send. A half-step, not the ~230 s the measurement points at. |
| `60f5872` | SD mount retried with the card's rail cut between attempts, instead of believing the first `SD.begin()`. |

Still open, in the pending-tasks note: the buoy does not *wait* for the pass to
open, so with a 120 s early wake the first messages go out below the floor; and
the attribution floor equals the operating floor, so those messages log no
satellite at all.
